I lead RL for robotics at [Luma AI](https://lumalabs.ai). Our recent work, [ZAP](https://zero-real.github.io/), turns a small open vision-language model into a robot policy trained entirely in simulation, with no real-robot data and no frontier teacher. ZAP matched GPT-6 Astra on real-world robots at 3× the speed and 1/17 the cost, and generalizes to new embodiments and tasks.

Before that, I was a core contributor to [Uni-1](https://lumalabs.ai/uni-1), Luma's unified model for image understanding and generation. I owned temporal reasoning and character consistency in pretraining and midtraining, from data through evaluation, teaching the model to keep characters and scenes consistent as a story moves through time. I was also responsible for RL for autoregressive and flow-matching generation, with a focus on legible text rendering.

Before Luma, I worked on data and evaluation for frontier labs' post-training at [Scale AI](https://scale.com), and published on reward-model robustness under distribution shift and on vision-language model calibration. I was then the founding engineer at Advex AI, a synthetic-data startup for industrial vision, where I led R&D on the core product: diffusion and flow-matching models that generate labeled images in data-poor environments.

Across all of it, I'm interested in how models generalize beyond what they were trained on, and how what a model learns in one modality carries into another.
